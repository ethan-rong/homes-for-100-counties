Homes for 100 Counties

What gets homes built in rural North Carolina, and how its growing cities can add homes, keep them affordable and keep neighbors in place.

The question this guide answers

What approaches can be used to promote new housing development in rural communities in North Carolina? How should urban communities meet fast-growing demand while maintaining affordability and preventing displacement?

Rural answer

Close the cost gap and name a lead.

The 78 rural counties permitted 40 homes per 1,000 residents since 2020, not counting manufactured homes; the six urban counties, 66.1 A statewide study found many rural markets have housing gaps that are large for their size.2 What is often missing is a project that pays for itself3 and someone to lead it.2

Answer 1 · Rural

Getting homes built in rural North Carolina

What approaches can be used to promote new housing development in rural communities in North Carolina?

The need is there. A statewide study found many rural and smaller markets have housing gaps that are large for their size, and that a lack of open apartments appears most common in rural areas.2

The obstacles are cost and capacity. Building can cost more than the finished home appraises for,3 and rural places are more likely to have no one leading housing work.2

Rural is not one thing. Thirteen rural counties grew faster than the state, led by Brunswick at 26.5%; twelve lost residents.1 Fast-growing rural counties should read the urban answer too.

First, name who leads.

In Our State, Our Homes, 14 local teams covering 22 counties5 reported 27 policy or zoning changes and $16.5 million committed in 18 months.6 Money alone is not enough: in April 2024 Dare County voted to return $35 million in state housing funds, citing developers' inability to make progress.7

R1

Offer a site and recruit the developer

Do the early work a developer will not: pick a site, test what could be built, then seek a partner. UNC's Development Finance Initiative helps local governments do this.89

In North Carolina
Rocky Mount, a city in two rural counties, worked with the initiative from 2017 to pick a downtown site and choose a developer.9 Five Points Crossing, 50 affordable apartments, was finished in February 2024 with a $4.9 million state recovery award.10
First step
List the land your town, county and school board own. Selling below market value has rules.
R2

Pay for the pipes and streets

Raw land is not a building site. Public money for water, sewer and streets can make a parcel buildable.2

In North Carolina
Golden LEAF's Open Grants, up to $500,000, can pay for infrastructure for nonprofit housing developments in rural, tobacco-dependent and economically distressed communities.11 In Goldsboro, a city in rural Wayne County, a $500,000 grant supports streets and utilities for a Habitat for Humanity neighborhood. The first phase is 31 homes; five were under construction in July 2026.12
First step
Ask your utility which sites have capacity. Price the extension to the best one.
R3

Stack state and federal financing

Rents that local workers can pay often will not cover the cost of new apartments. Federal housing tax credits and state loans, awarded by the NC Housing Finance Agency, exist to make such projects financially feasible.13

In North Carolina
The agency splits credits among four regions and generally allows one new-construction award per county outside the metro region. When funded, its Workforce Housing Loan Program can add a 0% loan of up to $3 million in low-income counties.13 The 2026 awards include apartments in Yadkinville (46), Sylva (60), Forest City (56), Mount Airy (64) and Lumberton (66).14 After Hurricane Helene, the state awarded $69 million for 828 rental homes in 10 western counties.15 For buyers, USDA direct loans can cut the interest rate to as low as 1%.16
First step
Meet a developer experienced with housing tax credits well before the yearly application. Bring a site or local money.
R4

Build for a known tenant

Start with an employer that cannot hire because workers have nowhere to live. A school district or county can anchor a small project.

In North Carolina
In Bertie County, 24 apartments for teachers and public workers opened in July 2024 with a $3.1 million interest-free loan from the SECU Foundation.17 County staff told researchers that no teacher living there had left.18
First step
Ask schools and large public employers how many job offers are turned down over housing. Public subsidy is generally limited by income.
R5

Reuse a school or a mill

An empty school or mill already has a site and a structure, and historic tax credits pay part of the cost.

In North Carolina
In Yanceyville, the former Dillard School became 53 affordable apartments for older adults, using federal senior-housing funds and historic tax credits. Leasing began in 2025.19 Marion's Clinchfield Mill has financing awards to become 141 apartments.14
First step
List empty public buildings and check which could be certified historic. The state credit is 15% of the first $10 million of eligible costs, plus 5% in tier 1 and 2 counties, and ends for costs incurred after 2029.20
R6

Use factory-built homes

Nationally, a basic single-section manufactured home costs about 35% as much to build as a comparable site-built home, before land.21

In North Carolina
Manufactured homes are 11% of the state's homes and more than a third in Warren, Gates, Robeson and Greene counties.1
First step
Read your zoning ordinance to see where manufactured homes are allowed. State law limits how far zoning can exclude them.

Who to call Development Finance Initiative · NC Housing Finance Agency · Golden LEAF Foundation · USDA Rural Development, North Carolina

Answer 2 · Urban

Growing without pushing people out

How should urban communities meet fast-growing demand while maintaining affordability and preventing displacement?

The six urban counties grew 9% from 2020 to 2025 and permitted 66 homes per 1,000 residents, more than suburban or rural counties. About half of their renters still pay 30% or more of income on rent.1

Research supports building: large new apartment buildings in low-income areas lowered nearby rents by about 6%.22 A review of the evidence found new supply does not raise displacement of lower-income residents.23 It does not reach everyone: in a statewide survey, 6% of market-rate apartments were vacant but only 1% of income-restricted ones, which had 41,702 households on waiting lists.2

Job 1 · Meet demand

Allow more homes, of more kinds

U1

Allow duplexes, townhouses and cottages

Let duplexes, townhouses, small apartment buildings and backyard cottages go where only detached houses were allowed.

In North Carolina
Raleigh did this in 2021 and 2022.24 By July 2026 the changes accounted for 4,936 homes under review, approved or built, 966 of them built.25 Charlotte has allowed duplexes and triplexes since June 2023 in single-family neighborhoods not bound by covenants or homeowner associations.26
First step
Count the homes your zoning allows on a typical lot near jobs. If the answer is one, start there.
U2

Zone for apartments where growth should go

Small infill adds homes slowly. Apartment buildings on main streets and near transit add many at once.

Evidence
Minneapolis grew its housing stock 12% from 2017 to 2022 while rents rose 1%. Most new homes were in large apartment buildings, not duplexes or triplexes.27
First step
Map corridors with bus service and sewer capacity. Rezone them for apartments ahead of time.

Job 2 · Maintain affordability

Pay for homes that stay affordable

U3

Raise local money

Voter-approved bonds and housing trust funds help pay for homes that agree to stay affordable.

In North Carolina
Charlotte's Housing Trust Fund has put $231.2 million toward more than 11,000 affordable homes.28 Raleigh voters approved a $101.5 million housing bond on November 3, 2026, by unofficial results.29 Bonds can fail: Cary's lost narrowly in 2024.30
First step
Put two numbers on one page: what a bond costs a typical household each year, and how many homes it funds.
U4

Build on public land

Land is a cost a local government can lower. Offer public sites to builders who commit to affordable homes.

In North Carolina
Chapel Hill broke ground in March 2026 on about 90 affordable homes on 14 acres the town owns,31 and is developing 48 affordable apartments on a second town site.32
First step
Map public parcels near jobs and transit. Rank them by readiness.
U5

Buy the affordable apartments you already have

Older apartments with modest rents can be lost when they are sold and repriced. A loan fund lets buyers who will keep rents low compete.

In North Carolina
The Wake Affordable Housing Preservation Fund, a $61 million loan fund from Wake County, Raleigh, three banks and Self-Help, has preserved 221 apartments with affordability held for 20 years.33
First step
List older complexes with modest rents. Meet the owners before they list.
U6

Use community land trusts

A land trust owns the land under a home and limits the resale price, so the home stays affordable for the next buyer.34

In North Carolina
Chapel Hill's Community Home Trust holds nearly 300 homes. Statewide, land trusts report 394 ownership homes and 398 rentals.35
First step
Find the land trust serving your region. Ask what it needs most: land, subsidy or staff.

Job 3 · Prevent displacement

Keep current residents in place

U7

Help owners stay

Tax-relief grants and repair loans aim to keep lower-income owners in their homes as values rise.

In North Carolina
Mecklenburg County's HOMES grant pays 35% of the county tax bill, up to $650, for owners earning up to 80% of the area's median income who have lived in the home three years.36 The state's Urgent Repair Program has reached more than 18,500 homes since 1994.37
First step
Find where assessed values rose most in the last countywide reappraisal. Send applications there first.
U8

Help renters stay

Eviction diversion gives a tenant a lawyer before the case reaches a judgment.

In North Carolina
Durham's program, run by Legal Aid of North Carolina with city funding, handled 840 cases in 2021. By the program's count, 80% closed without a judgment.38
First step
Get monthly eviction filings from the clerk of court. Fund legal help where they cluster.
U9

Target the neighborhoods at risk

Before a major rezoning or public investment, name the neighborhoods where residents are most exposed, and aim help there.

In North Carolina
Charlotte's 2023 Anti-Displacement Strategy sets that direction,39 and its Staying in Place pilot brings home repair and homebuying help to three neighborhoods.40 Orange County counted about 100 manufactured-home communities, home to more than 2,000 households, to plan ahead.41 This guide found no published results for either effort.
First step
Lay planned rezonings and transit projects over a map of renters, older owners and manufactured-home communities.

Ground rules

What North Carolina law allows

Cities and counties have only the powers the state gives them,42 and several changed in 2024 and 2026. This table summarizes statutes and UNC School of Government analysis as of November 2026. It is not legal advice.

AnswerCan a city or county…What to know

  1. Yes

    Sell public land to a chosen affordable-housing builder

    With deed limits keeping it for low- and moderate-income residents. A below-market price must use the powers state law gives housing authorities; a county using state or local tax money also needs a referendum.43

    G.S. 160D-1316(3), G.S. 157-9

  2. No

    Subsidize homes for households earning more than 80% of the area's median income

    Not a public purpose under current law, in the School of Government's analysis.43 No court has ruled on it.42

    G.S. 160D-1316(1), G.S. 157-3

  3. Generally no

    Waive development fees for affordable housing

    Pay a subsidy that covers the fees instead.42

    G.S. 160D-704

  4. Yes

    Borrow for housing with a bond

    Usually after a public vote.44 A mixed-income project must reserve at least 20% of units for low-income residents (city bonds) or 40% for low- or moderate-income residents (county bonds).

    G.S. 159-48, G.S. 159-49

  5. No

    Cap rents on private housing

    Except for owners who receive city or county funding or incentives, or who agree to rent limits on a subsidized property.42

    G.S. 42-14.1

  6. No

    Require landlords to accept federal housing vouchers

    Barred since September 9, 2024, except for owners who receive city or county funding or incentives.45

    G.S. 42-14.1(b)

  7. Unsettled

    Require affordable units in new development

    No statute authorizes it, and no North Carolina court has ruled on it.46 Commitments are likely allowed in a rezoning with site-specific conditions that the owner requests and agrees to in writing.47

    G.S. 160D-703(b)

  8. Not without consent

    Rezone land to allow fewer homes

    Every affected owner must agree in writing.48

    G.S. 160D-601(d)

  9. No, from 2027

    Require minimum parking

    Starts January 1, 2027. The 20 coastal counties and their towns are exempt, except for historic districts and landmarks.49

    G.S. 160D-702(c)(2a)

  10. No, in larger cities

    Ban backyard cottages

    From January 15, 2027, cities of 50,000 or more outside the coastal area must allow one with each single-family house, except in historic districts.50

    S.L. 2026-59, s. 44

  11. No, in the largest cities

    Keep homes off commercial land being redeveloped

    Since August 2026, cities of 80,000 or more in counties of 1 million or more must allow homes and apartments by right on commercial and light-industrial sites being redeveloped.50

    S.L. 2026-59, s. 43

  12. No

    Keep manufactured homes out

    Not from the whole jurisdiction and not by a home's age. Appearance, size and location can be regulated.51

    G.S. 160D-910

  13. No

    Create a local property-tax break

    Only the General Assembly can.52 A locally funded grant to lower-income owners is probably allowed.53

    G.S. 105-274; N.C. Const. art. V, § 2

How the numbers were made

County figures come from Census Bureau files, checked against separate Census products for all 100 counties with no mismatches. Homes permitted are units authorized from 2020 through 2025, divided by July 2020 population. Rent burden is the share of renters paying 30% or more of income on rent and utilities.

What an example proves

That an approach has been used here, and what its sponsor, a public record or a news report says about it. Not that it caused the result. Programs change, so check the source before you act.
